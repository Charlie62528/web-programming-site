History of the Internet

From packet switching and ARPANET to wireless access and IPv6: the development of the global network connecting computers and devices.

What is the Internet?

The Internet is a worldwide system that connects computers and other devices, enabling them to exchange information. It developed from early packet-switching research and networks such as ARPANET. TCP/IP, DNS, high-speed backbones, and wireless technologies transformed it from a research network into a global communication system.

Key milestones

1960s

Packet switching is developed

Paul Baran, Donald Davies, and Leonard Kleinrock developed foundational packet-switching concepts. Data is divided into small packets that may travel along different routes.

Why it mattered: Packet switching became the fundamental method for transmitting data across the Internet.

Source: Engineering and Technology History Wiki
1967

ARPANET is proposed

Larry Roberts published his plan for ARPANET after joining DARPA to develop the computer-network concept.

Why it mattered: ARPANET provided a practical foundation for connecting computers at different research institutions.

Source: Internet Society
1969

The first ARPANET message

On October 29, the first computer-to-computer signal crossed the four-node ARPANET between UCLA and the Stanford Research Institute.

Why it mattered: It demonstrated that geographically separated computers could communicate through a packet-switched network.

Source: DARPA
1971

Network email

Ray Tomlinson created network email and selected the “@” sign to separate the username from the destination address.

Why it mattered: Email became one of the earliest and most widely used network applications.

Source: Internet Hall of Fame
1974–1977

TCP connects different networks

Vinton Cerf and Robert Kahn designed TCP. A 1977 demonstration connected ARPANET with packet-radio and satellite networks.

Why it mattered: Independent networks could communicate as an interconnected “network of networks.”

Source: Internet Society
1983

ARPANET adopts TCP/IP

ARPANET completed its transition from NCP to TCP/IP on January 1.

Why it mattered: TCP/IP became the common standard that allowed different networks to communicate, marking the beginning of the modern Internet.

Source: Internet Society
1983–1985

DNS and the first .com domain

Paul Mockapetris designed the Domain Name System. In 1985, symbolics.com became the first registered second-level .com domain.

Why it mattered: DNS replaced an unscalable host-table system with memorable domain names, supporting the Internet’s global growth.

1986–1995

NSFNET expands Internet access

NSFNET connected academic researchers to supercomputer centers. The number of connected networks grew from 217 in 1988 to more than 50,000 in 1995.

Why it mattered: NSFNET supported the transition from a government-funded research network to a commercial and publicly accessible Internet.

Source: Merit Network
1989–1993

The World Wide Web opens the Internet

Tim Berners-Lee proposed the Web at CERN. The first browser and server were working by 1990, and CERN released the Web software openly in 1993.

Why it mattered: The Web made Internet information easier for ordinary users to access and navigate.

Source: CERN
1997–1998

Wi-Fi and Internet coordination

The IEEE published the 802.11 wireless standard in 1997. ICANN formed in 1998 to coordinate critical Internet identifiers.

Why it mattered: Wi-Fi enabled wireless access, while ICANN helped maintain one coordinated global Internet.

Source: ICANN
1998–2007

Search, participation, and mobile access

Google formed in 1998, Wikipedia launched in 2001, Facebook followed in 2004, and Apple introduced the iPhone in 2007.

Why it mattered: People could search, collaboratively create information, communicate socially, and access the Internet from mobile devices.

2012

World IPv6 Launch

Internet providers and websites were encouraged to enable IPv6 permanently.

Why it mattered: IPv6 provides a much larger address supply as the available IPv4 address space becomes insufficient.

Source: World IPv6 Launch

Three key Internet technologies

Packet switching

Divides data into small packets that can travel through different routes across a network.

TCP/IP

A set of communication protocols that allows different networks and devices to communicate.

Domain Name System

Translates domain names into IP addresses, making Internet resources easier to access by name.
