Introduction

The internet is a worldwide system that connects computers and other devices, enabling them to exchange information. Its development goes back to early research on packet switching and computer networks such as ARPANET. Over the years, the introduction of technologies like TCP/IP and DNS, and high-speed network infrastructure transformed it from a research network to the global communication system.

Key Milestones in the History of the Internet

1960s - Creation of Packet Switching

Paul Baran, Donald Davies, and Leonard Kleinrock developed foundational packet-switching concepts. Packet switching divides data into small packets that can travel through different routes. It became the fundamental method used to transmit data across the Internet

Packet Switching - Engineering and Technology History Wiki

1966 - Development of ARPANET

In late 1966 Roberts went to DARPA to develop the computer network concept and quickly put together his plan for the “ARPANET”, publishing it in 1967. ARPANET provided the practical foundation for connecting computers at different research institutions.

A Brief History of the Internet - Internet Society

1969 - First Computer to computer Connection

In its earliest form, the ARPANET began with four computer nodes, and the first computer-to-computer signal on this nascent network was sent between UCLA and the Stanford Research Institute on Oct. 29, 1969

ARPANET | DARPA

1971 - Introduction of Email

Raymond Tomlinson is widely known for inventing network electronics mail, choosing the “@” sign in emails to connect the username with the destination address. Email quickly became one of the earliest and most widely used network applications.

Raymond Tomlinson - Internet Hall of Fame

1973 - Development of TCP/IP

Robert Kahn and Vinton Cerf are credited with the initial design of the Transmission Control Protocol (TCP), thousands if not millions of others have contributed to the hundreds of protocols, thousands of networks, millions of applications, and billions of devices that make up the global Internet. TCP made it possible for different computer networks to communicate, introducing the idea of an interconnected “network of networks.”

Milestones:Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) Enables the Internet, 1974 - Engineering and Technology History Wiki

1977 - Packet-Radio Networks and Satellite Networks

The original ARPANET grew into the Internet. Internet was based on the idea that there would be multiple independent networks of rather arbitrary design, beginning with the ARPANET as the pioneering packet switching network, but soon to include packet satellite networks, ground-based packet radio networks and other networks.

A Brief History of the Internet - Internet Society

1982 - Transition to TCP/IP from NCP

The goal is to make a complete switch over from the NCP to IP/TCP by 1 January 1983.It is the task of each host organization to implement IP/TCP for its own hosts. This implementation task must begin by 1 January 1982.

A Brief History of the Internet - Internet Society

1983 - Domain Name System (DNS)

Paul Mockapetris invented the Domain Name System (DNS), designing its distributed architecture, query/response protocols, and naming hierarchy. DNS replaced the unscalable host-table system, enabling global growth of the Internet. His work underpins email, the Web, IP routing, and virtually every modern Internet service. This is frequently considered the birth of the modern Internet because TCP/IP became the common communication standard connecting different networks.

Paul Mockapetris - Who Made the Internet?

1985 - The first second-level dot com domain

Thirty years ago in 1985, the Internet was in its infancy. Apple's personal computer, the Macintosh, had only been on the market for a year, and the online service that eventually became AOL was readying its launch. It was during this time, on March 15th, that the first second-level dot com domain, symbolics.com, was introduced online, marking the beginning of the modern Internet.

Celebrating the Rise of the Modern Internet: The First Dot Com Domain Name Turns 30

1986 - NSFNET Launch

One of the most significant TCP/IP-based networks was NSFNET, launched in 1986 by NSF to connect academic researchers to a new system of supercomputer centers. NSFNET greatly expanded academic access and became an important high-speed backbone of the developing Internet.

Birth of the Commercial Internet - NSF Impacts | NSF - U.S. National Science Foundation

1989 - The World Wide Web

Tim Berners-Lee, a British scientist, invented the World Wide Web (WWW) in 1989, while working at CERN. The web was originally conceived and developed to meet the demand for automated information-sharing between scientists in universities and institutes around the world.

The birth of the Web – Home | CERN

1990 - First Web Browser

By the end of 1990, Tim Berners-Lee had the first Web server and browser up and running at CERN, demonstrating the ideas behind the proposal. The Internet began changing from a largely technical and academic network into an accessible information system.

The birth of the Web – Home | CERN

1993 - World Wide Web Commercialization

On 30 April 1993 CERN put the World Wide Web software in the public domain. CERN made the next release available with an open license, a surer way to maximize its dissemination. These actions allowed the web to flourish.

The birth of the Web – Home | CERN

1993 - Mosaic Browser

From January 1993, the National Center for Supercomputing Applications (NCSA) at the University of Illinois, provided pre-releases of its Mosaic browser for the Unix X Window System. Free standards encouraged worldwide adoption, while Mosaic made the Web easier for ordinary users by displaying text and images together.

The birth of the Web – Home | CERN

1995 - NSFNET Retirement

From 217 networks connected in July of 1988 to more than 50,000 in April of 1995 when the NSFNET backbone service was retired, the NSFNET’s exponential growth stimulated the expansion of the worldwide Internet and provided a dynamic environment for the development of new communications technologies. This represented an important transition from a mainly government-supported research network to a commercial, publicly accessible Internet

NSFNETBackbone Service

1997 - Wi-Fi

Wi-Fi first came onto the market in 1997 when the pioneering IEEE 802.11 technical standard was published, enabling wireless data transmission at up to 2 Mbit/s using an unlicensed 2.4 GHz radio spectrum. Wi-Fi allowed devices to connect to networks without physical cables, eventually making household and mobile Internet access much easier.

IEEE SA - The Evolution of Wi-Fi Technology and Standards

1998 - ICANN

ICANN is also working to ensure the Universal Acceptance of all domain names, regardless of language or script, in all Internet-enabled devices so everyone can communicate using a domain name and email address that best suits their needs. ICANN was formed in 1998 and its history traces back to the creation and evolution of the Internet itself. ICANN provided a new international coordination structure for critical Internet identifiers.

ICANN - About ICANN

1998 - Founding of Google

In August 1998, Sun co-founder Andy Bechtolsheim wrote Larry and Sergey a check for $100,000, and Google Inc. was officially born. Search engines made the rapidly growing Web easier to navigate.

How we started and where we are today - Google - About Google

2001 - Launch of Wikipedia

In 2001 Nupedia's wiki launches. And after 5 days Wikipedia launches at Wikipedia.com, after Nupedia's Advisory Board expresses concern about a Wiki being associated with Nupedia. It demonstrated how large communities could collaboratively create and distribute knowledge online

Wikipedia timeline - Meta-Wiki

2004 - Launch of Facebook

When Facebook launched in 2004, it changed the way people connect. Apps like Messenger, Instagram and WhatsApp further empowered billions around the world. Internet users increasingly became creators and participants instead of simply reading published webpages

Company Info

2007 - Introduction of the iPhone

On January 9, 2007, Apple introduced the iPhone, combining a mobile phone, a widescreen iPod with touch controls, and an Internet communication device into one handheld product. Its multi-touch interface and innovative software transformed how users interacted with mobile technology. “iPhone is a revolutionary and magical product that is literally five years ahead of any other mobile phone,” said Steve Jobs, Apple’s CEO.

Apple Reinvents the Phone with iPhone - Apple

2012 - IPv6 Adoption

In 2012, World IPv6 Launch encouraged Internet providers and websites to enable IPv6 permanently. IPv6 provided a vastly larger supply of Internet addresses because the older IPv4 address space was becoming insufficient.

World IPv6 Launch

Three Key Technologies of the Internet

Packet Switching

Packet switching divides data into small packets that can travel through different routes across a network.

TCP/IP

TCP/IP is a set of communication protocols that allows different networks and devices to communicate with each other.

DNS

DNS translates domain names into IP addresses, making websites easier to access by name.
